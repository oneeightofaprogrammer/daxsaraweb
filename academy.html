Academy

A place to become someone, not a place to be graded.

The premise

Daxsara Labs is a three-month program for teenagers built around one belief: a young person changes fastest when they are building something real, in a room with no fixed idea of who they're supposed to be.

It runs for three months rather than a full school year on purpose — long enough to move from a question to a finished thing, short enough that it always feels like it's ending somewhere. Each cohort moves through the same shape: a beginning that opens a question, a middle that gets hands dirty, and a close where the work is shown publicly, to family, mentors, and strangers walking by.

Why not a school

The room teaches, whether anyone intends it to.

Our first version of this program ran inside a university. It didn't work — not because of the people, but because a university already carries a grade economy, an inherited authority, and a fixed idea of what a student is. A teenager walks in already knowing the shape they're expected to take.

Daxsara Labs now runs inside Hedish, a shopping mall reimagined as a public stage for transformation — a space with no academic memory, where a teenager isn't yet anyone in particular. It is a deliberate choice, made after studying how malls elsewhere have become identity and community spaces rather than just retail.

How it's held together

Four systems, one program.

The Labs are the core engine — the actual three-month transformation. Around it sit three supporting systems: a knowledge infrastructure that keeps institutional memory as each cohort teaches us something new; a community of graduates and mentors that keeps the relationship alive after the program ends; and a public presence inside Hedish, so the transformation stays visible to anyone passing through, not hidden behind a closed door.
